Case report – fireball of 15 February 2013

Analysis rests on two viewpoints that could be oriented; a third viewpoint was set aside because its sight-lines were only 7.5 degrees from one of the others and its inclusion worsened the agreement. The remaining pair view the path with a wide separation of 66.3 degrees and tracked the object for 13.7 seconds, so the geometry is solid.

The solved trajectory runs from 54.427 N, 64.101 E at 89.5 kilometers altitude to 54.914 N, 60.615 E at 11.4 kilometers, moving toward 282.12 degrees, about west-northwest, on a shallow slope of 17.48 degrees. Speed is 19.65 kilometers per second with an uncertainty of 0.29 kilometers per second; companion estimate 19.07 kilometers per second confirms consistency. Because even the lower bound exceeds Earth escape speed, a natural object is solid.

Uncertainty remains small on slope and exact endpoint. Missing are later dark-flight data and recovered fragments, so final descent and strewn field are not constrained.
